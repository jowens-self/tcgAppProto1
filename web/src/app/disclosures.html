Where prices come from

  • Every price is TCGplayer's market price, which TCGplayer works out from recent sales. We don't calculate it. We collect it once a day from tcgcsv.com, a free, independent copy of TCGplayer's price data, and store each day's price exactly as it was. Stored prices are never edited afterwards. CardTracker isn't affiliated with TCGplayer or tcgcsv.
  • TCGplayer's data updates once a day. Until that day's collection has run, the newest prices shown are the previous day's. Dates are calendar days in UTC.
  • History goes back to February 2024 for Pokemon and One Piece. A day that wasn't collected is missing, never filled in with a guess. The one exception is Collection's value chart, which carries the last price forward (see below).
  • If a game's newest prices are {{ staleAfterDays() ?? 2 }} or more days old, every screen shows a warning that the numbers are not current.

What a price means

  • One printing, not one card. Normal, Holofoil, Reverse Holofoil, 1st Edition and so on trade separately, so each has its own price. They are never averaged together.
  • No condition. Prices aren't broken down by condition. Collection records the condition you enter, but values the card at the same market price whatever the condition.
  • TCGplayer's "high" price is never used, anywhere. Sellers can list at any price they like, including far above what anyone pays.
  • Market price only. Every screen uses the market price alone. TCGplayer's mid and low prices are what sellers are asking, not what anyone paid, so on a card that rarely sells they can sit far from what it actually goes for. They're never charted or counted. A day with no market price is skipped.
  • Digital code cards aren't tracked. They are online redemption codes packed with sealed product, not cards.

Movers

  • A move compares the median price of the last 3 days with the median of the 3 days ending at the start of the window (7 days ago, by default). A median rather than a single day or an average, so one odd sale can't top the board. For 1- and 2-day windows the medians shrink to fit, so the two ends never share a day.
  • A card is only compared if it has a market price on every one of those days.
  • The starting price must be at least the "Min $" amount ($1 unless you change it). Without it, a card going from 6 cents to 1 cent would top the drops at −83%.
  • Singles only, unless "Include sealed" is ticked.
  • Pre-release, early and flipping prices are left out unless ticked; see what trends leave out.
  • "Modern only": {{ modernRule() }}
  • The board shows the 50 biggest moves.

Sets

  • Value adds up the market price of every single printing in the set that has a market price on the newest day, one copy of each. Sealed product never counts toward a set.
  • Change compares the same cards at the start and end of the window (30 days by default). Only cards priced on both days count, so cards added to the set partway through can't look like a price move. Launch pricing and flipping prices are left out of the change but still count toward value.
  • "Correct one-day price errors" (on by default) fixes a reading that jumps and comes straight back. When a day's price is at least 25% away from the days either side of it, those two days agree within 5%, and the jump is worth at least $10, the median of the three days is used instead. Stored prices aren't changed, every set with a correction says so, and unticking the box shows the raw comparison. The newest day can't be corrected, because the day after it hasn't happened yet.
  • "Driven by" names the card whose price moved the most in dollars, and its share of all the set's movement, counting rises and falls alike. Past 50%, one card is doing most of the work and the set's percentage says more about that card than about the set, so it's flagged.
  • "Hide sets with no return" hides sets with no change to show: nothing priced on both days, or a new set with nothing settled yet. The screen says how many new sets it's hiding.

A card's page

  • The chart shows the market price day by day. A day with no market price is skipped, and the line joins the days either side of it. Ranges like "90d" count back from the card's newest price, not from today. A printing that has never had a market price has no chart.
  • Launch pricing is shaded. If it's unticked it's also left off the chart, unless the card has nothing else yet, in which case it's shown and labelled.
  • The change above the chart is from the first to the last point drawn.
  • The all-time high is the highest market price ever recorded for that printing, or a dash if it has never had one.
  • Pictures come from pokemontcg.io, a community project, and are stored with us. About 1 in 20 Pokemon cards, and every One Piece card, has no picture. A picture with a warning band shows a different printing of the card, because our source doesn't have this one; the prices are still this printing's. In search results such a card says "Base printing's art". About 1 in 5 pictures are borrowed this way.
  • A card whose price keeps flipping says so under its chart, with the two prices.

Collection

  • A card's value is the quantity you hold times its market price from the newest day we have. Condition isn't taken into account.
  • A card with no market price that day isn't counted, and the screen says how many were left out.
  • "Value on" a past date moves both what you held and the prices back to that day, so it shows what your collection was actually worth then.
  • The value chart carries each card's last price forward over a day with no data, rather than dropping the card to zero.
  • Cost and gain use the price you entered. A card with no price entered is left out of the cost and has no gain shown, rather than a gain of its whole value.
  • Nothing you record is overwritten. A change or a sale is a new entry, so past dates keep their answers.

What trends leave out

Movers and a set's change can be thrown off by prices that don't reflect where a card trades. Three kinds are left out by default. Each has a box on Movers, Sets and in Settings to bring it back. None is ever left out of a value: what a card or set is worth today counts every price.

Pre-release prices

Prices from before a card is released, mostly preorders, which usually sit well above where the card settles. A card's release is its set's release date. A card first priced more than 30 days after its set came out, like a new promo added to a long-running promo set, is released on its first price instead. {{ shown(prefs.includePreRelease()) }}

Flipping prices

Some cards that rarely sell have a market price that jumps between two far-apart prices and sits at each for days or weeks. One card went back and forth between about $250 and $10,000 a dozen times in six months. Either end of a comparison can land on the wrong one. A card is marked as flipping when, over the last 180 days:

  • its price jumped to at least double, or at most half, the day before;
  • stayed at the new price for two days or more;
  • then came back to within 25% of a price it had held for two days or more;
  • did that at least twice;
  • and the higher of its two prices is $1 or more.

A real price move steps once and stays, so it isn't marked. A card that never reaches $1 isn't marked either: it's under Movers' $1 minimum at both prices, and it moves a set's change by cents. The list is rechecked every day. Charts always show these prices as recorded. {{ shown(prefs.includeFlipping()) }}
